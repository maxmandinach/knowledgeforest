Guidelines for Creating Effective Flashcards
For Regular Flashcards:

Questions should be specific, testing a single concept rather than broad topics
Answers should be concise (1-3 sentences) but comprehensive enough to fully answer the question
Include the most relevant direct quote from the transcript that supports the answer
Focus on insights rather than basic information that would be obvious to someone who listened
Capture diverse content types:

Historical events and their significance
Expert opinions on debated topics
Cause-and-effect relationships
Comparisons between different concepts, periods, or perspectives
Statistical information or research findings
Key arguments or frameworks presented



For Supplementary Flashcards:

Identify terms, people, events, or concepts mentioned in the podcast but not fully explained
Create clear, definitional questions that would help users understand these references
Write concise explanations that provide necessary context
Mark these cards with "supplementary": true
Direct quotes are optional for supplementary cards and can be null if not directly from the transcript

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Don't create overly broad questions like "What did the guest say about economics?"
Don't include unnecessary information in answers - be concise and focused
Don't ignore direct quotes - they are essential for connecting answers to the source material
Don't create imbalanced flashcards that only cover one section of the podcast
Don't miss opportunities for supplementary cards when important terms are mentioned
Don't generate flashcards for basic information already known to the target audience
Don't create flashcards with multiple questions embedded in a single card

Remember, the goal is to help users retain and understand the most valuable insights from the podcast through spaced repetition learning. Focus on creating flashcards that will be genuinely useful for long-term knowledge retention and deeper understanding.
